[THE HM MARK]

Three bars. One shared stroke.

The H and M in this mark share a single vertical bar — bar 2 is simultaneously the right arm of H and the left arm of M. Neither letter exists independently. Remove bar 2 and both collapse.

That is not an accident. Brand systems work the same way. The visual identity, the product UX, the pitch deck — each depends on a shared structural logic. Separate them and they lose coherence.

The kanji stroke vocabulary was chosen deliberately — not for decoration, but because Mincho (明朝体) typography represents the highest form of thick-thin discipline: every stroke carries a reason for its weight.

Stroke anatomy

Bar 1 H left vertical
Bar 2 SHARED — H right = M left
Bar 3 M right vertical
Crossbar 3 px thin — Mincho 1:3.3 ratio
Uroko 鱗 Triangular serif at each bar base
M diagonal 10 px → 6 px tapered at V-peak
Tome 止 Downward wedge at crossbar end

Why these two characters

Harussani Manaphassan is not a Japanese name. But 晴 carries the first phoneme of Harussani, and 真 carries the first phoneme of Manaphassan. The initials H and M collapse into two characters with real meaning in a 2,000-year-old writing system.

More than phonetics: 晴 (clarity) and 真 (truth) are the two forces that have governed every project in this portfolio. The pitch deck that secured RM100,000 was clear and verifiable. The JomParking brand identity that won a national award was structurally honest. The telehealth UX was not embellished — it was mapped to actual user journeys.

晴真 is not branding. It is a description.

  • —The H crossbar in this mark is 3 px. The bars are 10 px. That ratio is not arbitrary — it is a decision.

[THE PAPER · E-INK AESTHETIC]

Not nostalgia. A commitment.

The warm off-white background (#F5F0EA) has approximately 3,000 years of optimisation behind it. Paper is not retro — it is the most readable substrate ever invented. This portfolio uses it deliberately.

E-ink displays represent what I want designed work to feel like: extremely high contrast, zero noise, every pixel intentional. No gradients for the sake of depth. No shadows for the sake of modernity. Light where it must be light. Dark where it must be dark.

I build digital products but I think in print. The same discipline that prevents a pre-press operator from wasting a plate is the discipline that prevents a designer from adding a decorative element that serves no hierarchy.
